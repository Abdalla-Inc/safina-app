# Life Reset: 66 Day Habit — product study

**Research date:** 27 September 2026 (Australia/Brisbane). **Identity:** [Life Reset: 66 Day Habit on the Australian App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/life-reset-66-day-habit/id6478942469), formerly Rise, by Savvy Tech Limited. Australian listing displays iOS version **6.5.5**; Google Play shows an update dated 23 September 2026. This is the likely app the founder downloaded, although I have not seen their installed screen. This report observes the developer's public help pages, store listings and website, plus a historical third-party onboarding capture. I did **not** run the installed app or subscribe. Described controls may differ by platform, account, experiment or version. It does not prove that Life Reset improves habits, and it makes no design decision for Safinat Al-Nur.

## Executive picture
Life Reset packages self-improvement as a bounded **66-day personal transformation**. It asks about the person's goals, builds a tailored daily task program, then repeatedly ties real-world tasks to a game layer: XP, ratings, streaks, achievements, a character, gear, boss fights and sometimes season rankings. The Home screen puts Day X/66, today's tasks and progress together. A journal and historical stats add reflection. It attempts return through visibility (widgets), milestones, a clear next day's tasks, social accountability and, for opt-in Hard Mode, loss penalties. Its subscription begins at the end of personalized onboarding; public listings say onboarding is free but daily program features require payment. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/life-reset-66-day-habit/id6478942469) · [Home help](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355208-exploring-the-life-reset-home-page) · [Program help](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355468-66-day-life-reset-program).

**Most distinctive observation for our later conversation:** this is not merely a checklist with a streak. It layers a **guided arc**, personalized plan, escalating visible identity, everyday task cards, separate modes and long-term milestones around the same repeated behavior. The exact effectiveness of each layer is unknown.

### Daily interface and progress accounting
[Life Reset's Home guide](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355208-exploring-the-life-reset-home-page) describes the main shell: program day, current streak, achievements and rating at top; To-dos/Done/Skipped task filters; task name, frequency, difficulty and streak badge on cards; Home, Stats, Journal, Seasons, Friends and Mini Tools below. This is an **information hierarchy**: immediate action and long-term journey coexist. A task can be intentionally skipped rather than only left incomplete. Stats includes historical data, streak records and completion rates. Journal supports daily feelings/thoughts; the website also says media can be attached. [Website](https://www.lifereset.com/).

The documented streak is unusually specific: **at least two tasks completed on a day** secure it. Fewer than two do not. Achievements can unlock from streak lengths, rating levels and program days such as 7, 33 and 66. The rating view contrasts Day 1, Current and potential Day 66; the algorithm is not published in the help article. [Help](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355441-streaks-achievements-ratings). This is a progress story constructed from **self-checked tasks**; the public material does not show independent verification that the real-world action happened.

The [66-day program help](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355468-66-day-life-reset-program) says a new program day begins at **4:00 AM in the selected timezone**, avoiding a midnight cutoff. Members can alter tasks' intensity, frequency and schedule in Settings → Edit Program; edits affect future days. The program cannot be paused, but it can be restarted and past program data remains reviewable. Store release notes for 6.5.0 describe a “stuck on a habit” question flow leading to a reminder, bedtime countdown or short tool session, a first-task celebration, a smoother Day 1 and color-coded difficulty. These are **release notes**, not a walkthrough of the current flow. [AU App Store release history](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/life-reset-66-day-habit/id6478942469).

### Game and return layers
- [Character Mode](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355321-character-mode): optional RPG framing with Discipline, Focus, Strength, Confidence and Wisdom stats; daily habits become quests; completing tasks yields XP and gear. Task completion rate affects win/loss of boss fights; character acts/reacts throughout the day. The exact encounter schedule and XP arithmetic are undocumented.
- [Motivation Cards](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355342-motivation-cards): one card per day from five categories, same card on repeat opening, refresh at 6 AM. Rarity tiers (Common, Rare, Master-Rare, X-Rare) change presentation. This provides a collectible surprise layer without infinite redraws.
- [Widgets](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355275-the-life-reset-widget): program day, task progress and streak are visible without opening the app; sync may lag. This is a persistent cue as well as a utility.
- [Friends](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355421-friends-and-social): invite code/link, friend requests, shared seasons and progress comparison, with “nudge” fixes in release notes. [Seasons](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355327-season-challenge) use timed global leagues, XP, tiers and leaderboards; people can see others' oaths and progress. This turns self-work into social comparison.
- [Hard Mode](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355290-hard-mode): optional high-stakes mode in which a missed day restarts Day 1, tasks cannot be edited and skipping penalizes the member, with one skip day allowed. **Documentation conflicts:** that article says Hard Mode can be turned off anytime; [Common Issues](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355297-common-issues) says it cannot be turned off manually and offers an exit after two failed days. Current installed behavior requires direct inspection.
- [Win Your Money Back](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13365697-win-your-money-back-program): a commercial commitment device. It claims covered subscription payments can be refunded after 66 days if ≥70% of tasks are completed and proof is emailed within seven days; subscription continues unless canceled. This is a published conditional offer, not proof of sustained habit formation.

### Friction, monetization and privacy
The app's public listing says free personalized assessment/program setup but a subscription for daily quests, XP, streaks, tools, Hard Mode, cards and widgets. The Australian listing exposes many **in-app purchase price points** rather than an unambiguous single plan duration; avoid quoting any as “the price.” [AU listing](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/life-reset-66-day-habit/id6478942469). An older third-party [ScreensDesign capture](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/rise-life-reset-in-66-days) shows the assessment, plan customization and commitment ritual before a paywall plus exit offer; it should not be mistaken for the current paywall. [Subscription help](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13271338-subscriptions-and-payments) says plans renew automatically and deleting the app/account does not cancel. The refund offer is separate from ordinary cancellation/refunds.

The Apple listing's developer-declared privacy label says contact information and identifiers **may be used to track across apps/websites**; Apple states it has not verified the declaration. It lists no accessibility features indicated and 17 languages that do **not** include Arabic. [AU App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/life-reset-66-day-habit/id6478942469). The website's [privacy policy](https://www.lifereset.com/privacy-policy) is broad; Google Play's developer declaration says certain data may be collected, encryption in transit and deletion requests. Do not infer the handling of a particular member's journal or health data from a generic label alone. [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=build.designand.riselife).

The [troubleshooting guide](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13366175-troubleshooting-bugs) says basic task completion works offline, while syncing progress/streaks, XP/stats and community need a connection. It recommends cloud and manual backups before reinstalling. [Account help](https://help.lifereset.com/en/articles/13355449-account-and-data-management) preserves past programs on restart. Store release notes in June 2026 refer to fixes for lost task progress, wrong goals/unscheduled tasks and sync reliability; those are evidence the team addressed reliability problems, not proof that all are resolved. [AU version history](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/life-reset-66-day-habit/id6478942469).

### User signals, with strong caveat
The AU App Store displayed roughly **4.6/5 from 1K ratings** and Google Play **4.4/5 from 8.7K reviews** at this inspection; figures change and review samples are not representative. Selected reviews praise visible stat points but complain about widget/XP bugs, incomplete plan fit and features behind payment; a Google reviewer reported program-date confusion. Treat these as leads for usability testing, not established prevalence or causal evidence. [AU listing](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/life-reset-66-day-habit/id6478942469) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=build.designand.riselife).

## Mechanism map for later Safinat discussion — **no adoption decision yet**
| Life Reset element | Mechanism it appears to seek | Question we should test for Qur’an reading |
|---|---|---|
| Tailored plan + easy first day | Personal relevance and approachable start | Can onboarding help choose a realistic level without a lengthy quiz or manufactured diagnosis? |
| Today task cards and Done/Skipped states | Reduce “what next?” friction; make actual action salient | Can exact B/juz checkmarks and partial ranges keep truthful history without encouraging blind ticking? |
| 66-day arc + milestones + rating | Make distant progress visible and memorable | How should our 30-credit ship and weekly/monthly khatmas tell a longer story without a false habit deadline? |
| Character and gear | Make identity and growth tangible | Could a gradually built ship and fleet deliver the useful visibility without role-playing spiritual worth? |
| Card rarity, achievements, leagues | Surprise, status and competition | Are these compatible with a calm reading practice and private record? Their value here is unproven. |
| Widget + tailored “stuck” response | Return cue and timely intervention | Which user-controlled cue helps actual reading, and when should it stop? |
| Hard Mode and refund stakes | Loss aversion and commitment pressure | Would this undermine honest partial logs or create shame? Founder has favored non-destructive ship progress. |

**Scientific caution:** the app's 66-day framing is a product arc. The original [Lally et al. habit study](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674) found wide variation (18–254 days to a modeled automaticity threshold) in other everyday behaviors and reported that one missed opportunity did not materially derail formation. It does not validate a guaranteed 66-day transformation, nor Life Reset's efficacy in Qur’an practice. The developer's user count, testimonials and “science-backed” description are marketing, not controlled outcome studies.

## What still needs direct observation
This public-source audit cannot establish the current in-app onboarding branches, task-check animation, actual default reminders, how XP is calculated, detailed stats calendar, partial task recording, whether a user can recover after a long absence, current Hard Mode exit, or paywall shown in the founder's account. A short **screen recording or screenshots from the installed app**, beginning with the home screen, a task completion, stats/calendar and any character/ship-like progress screens, would let us compare the parts the founder actually likes. The founder can share those later; this study is already useful without them. Do not use third-party historical screens as current 6.5.5 evidence.
