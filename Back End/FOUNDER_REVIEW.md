# Safinat Al-Nur — founder review v0.2

## Executive summary

**Your voice decisions are now reflected in the engine documents.** You are the sole reviewer; there is no coach approval step. I replaced the old 25-credit ship proposal and the assumption that Al-Baqarah must be read twice where it overlaps a juz. No app has been built, and no repository was provided for a source audit.

**What a member does:** choose a level → see their monthly group’s position or weekly khatma and next passage → read in real life → log exactly what they read, including partial or extra reading → see a private reading calendar and ship change → leave. One fully met personal day earns **one ship credit at every level**. **Thirty** credits complete a ship. A completed **khatma is a separate record** requiring the actual whole Qur’an; thirty B-only days can finish a ship without claiming a khatma. A miss never removes ship work. Partial credit proportional to recorded target reading remains a proposal to confirm.

**Two different clocks:** B+1, B+2 and B+3 juz groups follow the month. One-juz members start at J1 on the 1st and join at J16 on the 16th. Monthly members can switch levels **that day**, keep what they already read and join the new group’s current place. February must distribute any remaining portions before month-end. The **31st is free** for rest, catch-up or voluntary reading. B+4 and B+5 are **weekly Sunday–Saturday khatma** commitments. Four per day needs two extra juz somewhere in the week; five per day can finish in six days and leave Saturday free. Each Sunday starts a new khatma at J1 even after an incomplete Saturday. A weekly level change starts next Sunday.

**Overlap:** If Al-Baqarah contains today's assigned verses, reading it **once** counts there. At the start of a B+2 monthly plan, reading Al-Baqarah alone fulfils the first day, as you specified. Actual coverage is recorded once; no duplicate reading or credit is required. The app must preserve the exact last logged verse and show the next place to continue.

**Your remaining choices:** (1) Which February dates carry extra portions and exactly where B+2/B+3 resume after the day-one B overlap; (2) how a flexible B+4/B+5 member's chosen daily plan earns a full day credit while the weekly khatma is incomplete; (3) what a partly built ship looks like when a month/week ends, including whether credits carry into a continuing ship; (4) how same-day monthly changes combine old/new assignments into at most one credit; (5) confirm proportional partial credit and the exact Qur’anic verse text/context used around Nuh's ship. I have made these explicit rather than hardcoding an answer.

### Your B+2 new-member example
On the first day, they select B+2 and see Al-Baqarah as today's sufficient reading. They read it once, log 2:1–286, receive one full-day credit, and their monthly khatma coverage reflects what those actual verses cover. The next day the app shows the group's next published ranges, along with any relevant remaining partial passage, without asking them to reread yesterday's overlapping verses. If they only read half a later target, the log says what happened and proposed fractional ship credit is awarded. If they switch to B+1 on the 16th, today's new group position is J16; earlier same-day reading stays in history, and no act counts twice. The exact day-two boundary and same-day credit combination are awaiting your choice.

### Your B+5 weekly example
Sunday begins a new weekly khatma at J1. They log B once and enough further passages to cover their chosen five-juz pace, with overlapping verses represented once. The app shows both today's plan and what remains of the 30-juz week. Six fully completed five-juz days can complete the khatma; Saturday is available for catch-up or rest. On the following Sunday a fresh weekly khatma starts at J1 even if the prior week remains incomplete in their private history. If they request B+4 on Wednesday, that change takes effect Sunday. Daily ship credits are still capped at one, separate from the khatma.

## Ship, khatma and calendar
| Measure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Day credit | Keeping today's own approved reading commitment; full=1, partial fraction proposed, none=0 | 30 full B days = 30 credits. |
| Ship | One vessel at 30 day credits; its period story/carry presentation still open | B reader finishes a ship without a khatma. |
| Khatma | Whole Qur’an actually logged within its weekly/monthly cycle | Weekly five-juz reader can complete a khatma in six days, while earning at most six ship credits. |
| Private calendar | Tier color **with label/pattern** plus separate actual status; khatma mark if proven | Past dates keep their historical tier colors after a change. |

A calendar cell distinguishes complete, partial, supplemental-only, no entry, pause and free day. It can show a split tier on a same-day switch. A headline such as “22 reading days, 5 partial” describes actual log history; a conventional streak is optional and secondary, never a ship-damaging score. Colors identify commitments, **not spiritual rank**. The forest-like accumulating visual you cited is a useful comparison, but the ship does not wither after interruptions. Research and product audit: [HABIT_ENGINE_RESEARCH.md](HABIT_ENGINE_RESEARCH.md).

At 9/18/27 fully completed days in a 30-date illustration, any tier earns 9/18/27 credits; none completes a 30-credit ship. Thirty complete days finish one. If unused credits carry forward (recommended but unapproved), approximate completion at 90/60/30% evenly distributed full-day adherence takes 34/50/100 calendar days. A volume-per-juz model would greatly accelerate high tiers and leave B members behind. A partial-boost formula was considered but is less transparent. Full comparison and the seven-tier simulation: [PROGRESSION_MODELS.md](PROGRESSION_MODELS.md).

**Nuh story and ayat:** explore a 30-part ship with actual reading shaping the visual and a year of vessels forming a fleet. A B-only member's parts reflect repeated sincere days, **not falsely named Juz 1–30**. Al-Baqarah as an “engine” is a visual metaphor, not a religious claim about that surah. You mentioned ship-building and embarkation ayat; before display, you will choose the exact Arabic text, citation, translation/context and placement. The app should not claim its ship guarantees protection.
